      About the data

      What you’re looking at

      Every registered voter in New York City belongs to one election district, a small area of a few hundred to a couple thousand voters. Election districts fit exactly inside City Council, State Assembly, State Senate and Congressional districts, so this site adds them up into each kind of district. Everything here can be mapped, picked, compared and downloaded.

      Sources

      • Voters: NYS Board of Elections, enrollment by election district, party and status, as of February 20, 2026. 5,359,346 voters.
      • Election results: NYC Board of Elections certified results by election district for the November 2024 general, June 2025 primary, November 2025 general and June 2026 primary. Ranked-choice rounds for the June 2025 primary come from the Board’s cast vote records.
      • Residents: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates by census tract, and the Bureau’s citizen voting-age population special tabulation (2020–2024) by block group.
      • Homes: NYC Department of City Planning, PLUTO 26v2 tax lots; NYC Open Data building footprints; NYCHA development boundaries and Development Data Book.
      • Conditions: NYC Open Data: 311 service requests, HPD housing maintenance code violations, marshals’ evictions, and NYPD complaint data (historic and year to date).
      • Representatives: council.nyc.gov, nyassembly.gov, nysenate.gov and the Clerk of the U.S. House, checked against certified winners and OpenStates.
      • Poll sites: NYC Board of Elections poll site lists for the November 3, 2026 general election.
      • Money: NYC Council expense funding disclosure for fiscal year 2027; NYC Campaign Finance Board 2025 contributions; Federal Election Commission bulk individual contributions for 2025–2026.
      • Lines: NYC Department of City Planning election district releases (24c through 26c) and district lines. Address search: NYC Planning Labs GeoSearch. Basemap: Esri.

      Election results

      Each election is shown on the election district lines in force on its election day. The June 2026 primary used the same lines as this map. Earlier elections used older lines, so when you view them by election district the map switches to those lines and picking is turned off. Council, Assembly, Senate and Congressional lines did not change across these elections, so district totals line up with the rest of the site.

      A candidate who ran on more than one party line has those lines added together. When the Board combined small election districts for an election, the votes are reported under the host district and the others show the host’s numbers. Turnout compares ballots cast with the February 2026 voter roll, so it is only shown where the two line up in time and place: the June 2026 primary everywhere, and the November 2025 general for whole districts.

      Checks: every district total matches the Board’s certified recaps (27,335 Assembly-level and 14,873 county-level comparisons, no differences), and every ballot in a district race sits inside the district the Board assigned it to. The ranked-choice count reproduces the Board’s certified final round exactly for 37 of 38 contests; the Republican primary in Council District 47 was certified by hand count and is left out.

      Who lives here: Census data

      Tracts and election districts don’t line up, so each tract is split using 2020 Census blocks. A block’s residents go to the election district its center falls in. Eligible voters come from the Bureau’s official citizen voting-age tabulation, split the same way from block groups. Districts are the sum of their election districts.

      Every census number has a 90% margin of error, shown as ± in tables and downloads, and a reliability rating: high when the coefficient of variation is under 15%, medium from 15% to 30% or when most residents come from split tracts, low above 30%. Low numbers are faded on the map.

      Checks: tract totals equal the Bureau’s county figures; our Senate, Assembly and Congressional estimates match the Bureau’s own published district estimates (population within 2.5%, eligible voters within 1.5%); the website’s math is tested against an independent build with zero differences.

      Homes

      “Homes” counts residential units in PLUTO, the city’s tax lot file. Each lot is placed at its point on the map. Lots with several buildings, like Co-op City or Stuyvesant Town, are split across their buildings by footprint area times height. NYCHA developments are pooled and spread across every NYCHA building in the development, because PLUTO sometimes books a whole development on one lot. Four NYCHA lots with impossible counts (under 250 square feet per unit) use NYCHA’s own apartment count. Hotels, hospitals and nursing homes, shelters and dorms are left out because they are not homes. Every unit is placed. Totals track the 2020 Census closely (median election district within 2%), and the larger gaps are buildings finished since 2020.

      Conditions

      All conditions cover the same twelve months, July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026, because NYPD’s latest release ends June 30. Each record is placed by its location. NYPD records sit on street centerlines and corners, and election district lines run down streets, so a record within 25 feet of a line is shared equally among the districts there. Rates use 2020 Census counts: residents for 311 and crime, homes for housing violations and evictions. Places with few residents, like Midtown, show very high rates per resident.

      Representatives and poll sites

      Representatives come from each body’s official roster and are checked two ways: against the winners in our certified results and against OpenStates. Where they differ, the seat changed hands after that election (Council District 3, Senate Districts 22 and 47) or is vacant (Senate District 12, since August 2026). Poll sites are the Board’s lists for November 3, 2026. The Board created 71 new election districts for that election that are not on any city map yet; if your Assembly district is flagged, confirm your site at findmypollsite.vote.nyc.

      Money

      Council discretionary funding is every designation in the Council’s fiscal year 2027 disclosure, credited to the sponsoring member’s district. Speaker’s initiatives, borough delegations and citywide initiatives are kept separate. Donations are by the donor’s ZIP code, using the health department’s ZIP areas, which group a few small ZIP codes. City races are net of refunds. Federal totals count each gift once, under the committee that received it, including gifts made through ActBlue and WinRed. The FEC only lists donors who give a committee more than $200 in the cycle.

        Things to know

        • Party names: “No party” means voters not enrolled in any party. “Other parties” combines every party besides Democratic, Republican, Conservative and Working Families.
        • Address search uses simplified lines. For an address right on a line, confirm with the city’s poll site locator.
        • Election district 40047 in Queens (1 voter) has no published boundary. It counts toward Assembly 40 and Queens only.
        • 188 mapped election districts have no registered voters. They are parks, airports and similar areas, shown in gray.
