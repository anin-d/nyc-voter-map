    About the data

    What you’re looking at

    Every registered voter in New York City belongs to one election district, a small area of a few hundred to a couple thousand voters. Election districts fit exactly inside City Council, State Assembly, State Senate and Congressional districts, so this site adds them up into each kind of district. Search an address, click any place, compare places side by side, and download the numbers.

    Sources

    • Voters: NYS Board of Elections, enrollment by election district, party and status, as of February 20, 2026. 5,359,346 voters.
    • Election results: NYC Board of Elections certified results by election district for the November 2024 general, June 2025 primary, November 2025 general and June 2026 primary. Ranked-choice rounds for the June 2025 primary come from the Board’s cast vote records.
    • Residents: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2020 to 2024 5-year estimates by census tract, and the Bureau’s citizen voting-age population special tabulation (2020 to 2024) by block group.
    • Homes: NYC Department of City Planning, PLUTO 26v2 tax lots; NYC Open Data building footprints; NYCHA development boundaries and Development Data Book.
    • Neighborhood conditions: pulled from NYC Open Data’s API each week: 311 service requests, HPD housing code violations, marshals’ evictions and NYPD complaints.
    • Representatives: council.nyc.gov, nyassembly.gov, nysenate.gov and the Clerk of the U.S. House, checked against certified winners and OpenStates.
    • Poll sites: NYC Board of Elections poll site lists for the November 3, 2026 general election.
    • Money: NYC Council expense funding disclosure for fiscal year 2027; NYC Campaign Finance Board 2025 contributions; Federal Election Commission individual contributions for 2025 to 2026.
    • Lines: NYC Department of City Planning election district releases and district lines. Address search: NYC Planning Labs GeoSearch. Basemap: Esri.

    Election results

    Each election is shown on the election district lines in force on its election day. The June 2026 primary used the same lines as this map. Earlier elections used older lines, so when you view them by election district the map switches to those lines. Council, Assembly, Senate and Congressional lines did not change across these elections, so district totals line up with the rest of the site.

    A candidate who ran on more than one party line has those lines added together. When the Board counted small election districts together with a neighbor, the votes are reported under the host district. Turnout compares ballots with the February 2026 voter roll, so it is only shown where the two line up in time and place: the June 2026 primary everywhere, and the November 2025 general for whole districts.

    Checks: every district total matches the Board’s certified recaps, and every ballot in a district race sits in the district the Board assigned it to. The ranked-choice count reproduces the Board’s certified final round exactly for 37 of 38 contests; the Republican primary in Council District 47 was certified by hand count and is left out.

    Who lives here

    Tracts and election districts don’t line up, so each tract is split using 2020 Census blocks: a block’s residents go to the election district its center falls in. Eligible voters come from the Bureau’s official citizen voting-age tabulation, split the same way from block groups. Districts are the sum of their election districts.

    Every census number has a 90% margin of error, shown as ± in tables and downloads, and a reliability rating: high when the coefficient of variation is under 15%, medium from 15% to 30% or when most residents come from split tracts, low above 30%. Low numbers are marked and faded on the map.

    Homes

    Homes are residential units in PLUTO, the city’s tax lot file. Lots with several buildings, like Co-op City, are split across their buildings by footprint area times height. NYCHA developments are pooled and spread across every NYCHA building in the development, because PLUTO sometimes books a whole development on one lot; four NYCHA lots with impossible counts use NYCHA’s own apartment count. Hotels, hospitals and nursing homes, shelters and dorms are left out because they are not homes.

    Neighborhood conditions

    Each source covers its latest 12 complete months, so the windows can differ by source. A record with a tax lot is placed in the election district that holds the lot. Other records use their map point; NYPD points sit on street centerlines and corners, and election district lines run down streets, so a point within 25 feet of a line is shared equally among the districts there. Evictions without a location are placed by address when the house number, street, borough and ZIP code all match. Rates use 2020 Census counts: residents for 311 and crime, homes for housing violations and evictions. Places with few residents, like Midtown, show very high rates per resident.

    Representatives and poll sites

    Representatives come from each body’s official roster and are checked against the winners in our certified results and against OpenStates. Where they differ, the seat changed hands after that election (Council District 3, Senate Districts 22 and 47) or is vacant (Senate District 12, since August 2026). Poll sites are the Board’s lists for November 3, 2026. The Board created 71 new election districts for that election that are not on any city map yet; if your Assembly district is flagged, confirm your site at findmypollsite.vote.nyc.

    Money

    Council discretionary funding is every designation in the Council’s fiscal year 2027 disclosure, credited to the sponsoring member’s district. Speaker’s initiatives, borough delegations and citywide initiatives are kept separate. Donations are by the donor’s ZIP code, using the health department’s ZIP areas. City races are net of refunds. Federal totals count each gift once, under the committee that received it, including gifts made through ActBlue and WinRed. The FEC only lists donors who give a committee more than $200 in the cycle.
